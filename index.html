Biweekly Activity Report

Your calendar and sent mail already know where the fortnight went. This writes the diary, so you edit instead of remember.

Timesheet diary From calendar + sent mail Deterministic — no AI No data collection Free

Why

Every two weeks you have to account for your time, and by Friday you've forgotten Tuesday. So you scroll back through your own calendar reconstructing it — which is work about work, and it's the same reconstruction every time. The record already exists in two places you already own.

What it does

  • Builds the diary — day by day, from the meetings you attended and the substantive email you sent over the last two weeks.
  • Estimates hours sensibly — with a per-day cap, so three overlapping invitations don't report an eleven-hour day.
  • Filters to a project — subject keywords pull out one project's work specifically.
  • Hands off cleanly — copy as rich text, copy as plain text, or save straight to your Drafts.

What it isn't

It is a memory aid, not a timekeeping system of record. Hours are estimated from what your mailbox and calendar can see, so work done elsewhere — field time, a workshop, a long stretch in another application — won't appear. Read the draft and correct it; you're the one signing it.

Privacy by design

No servers, no accounts, no analytics, and no AI anywhere in it. The report is built inside your own Outlook session against Microsoft Graph — within the Microsoft 365 boundary — and stays there until you copy it or save it. We never see your email. Read the privacy policy.

Get it

Available on Microsoft AppSource. In Outlook, open Get Add-ins and search for Biweekly Activity Report. Questions: Matthew@purposebuilt.systems.
