Biweekly Activity Report — Tutorial

Every two weeks you have to account for where your time went, and by then you've forgotten. Your calendar and your sent mail already know. This builds the diary from them, so you're editing a draft instead of staring at a blank box.

Tutorial — your first report

Worked example: it's Friday, the report is due, and the last two weeks are a blur.

1. In Outlook, open the Activity Report ribbon group and click Biweekly Report.
2. Click Generate report.
You should see a day-by-day diary of the last 14 days: the meetings you attended and the substantive email you sent, grouped and attributed, with hours worked out.
3. Read it and fix it. This is a first draft of your memory, not a submission.
4. Send it onward: Copy for rich text (keeps the formatting — paste into Word or a form), Copy as text for plain text (for systems that reject formatting), or Save to Drafts to finish it as an email.

Tuning it — the options that actually matter

Defaults are sensible; you'll probably change two or three of these once and never again.

OptionWhat it's for
Days back14 for a normal biweekly. Raise it if you're catching up after leave.
Hours mode / Standard day (h) / Max day (h) How hours are estimated and capped. Max day is the guard rail — it stops a day of overlapping invitations reporting eleven hours because three meetings ran at once.
Work start / Work end Your normal hours, so a 9 p.m. email doesn't imply an evening shift.
Project nameThe label at the top of the report — set it to whatever your form expects.
Keywords Comma-separated, matched in subjects. Use these to pull out one project's work specifically — e.g. bridge, US-30, culvert.
Min/sent email, Min/received email Minutes credited per message. This is the dial that decides whether email reads as real work or as noise. Lower it if your totals look inflated.

Read this before you submit one

It is a memory aid, not a timekeeping system. The hours are estimated from what your calendar and mailbox show — an invitation you accepted and ignored counts as a meeting, and work done in a system that isn't Outlook doesn't appear at all. Always read the draft and correct it. You are the one signing it.
Two weeks look suspiciously empty? Most often the work happened somewhere the add-in can't see — a workshop, field time, a long stretch in another application. Add those by hand; the diary gives you the scaffolding, not the whole account.

How it decides what counts

The rules are deterministic — no AI is involved anywhere. It reads your calendar and your sent mail, filters out the trivial, credits time using the minutes-per-email settings above, and caps each day at your maximum. The same fortnight always produces the same report, which is what makes it safe to base a submission on.

Where your data goes

Nowhere. There is no publisher server. The report is built inside Outlook from your own mailbox and calendar, and it stays there until you copy it or save it to your Drafts.
